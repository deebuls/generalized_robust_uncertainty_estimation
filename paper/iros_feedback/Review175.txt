Reviewer 1 of IROS 2025 submission 2656

Comments to the author
======================

Negative log-likelihood often struggles with outliers, and
relying on sampling increases inference time. According to
the paper, the proposed algorithm can choose a suitable
distribution depending on the presence of outliers, which
helps address this issue. The suggestion and limitation are
listed below:

1. the comparison with other algorithms seems a bit limited
— it only considers a few different distributions. It's
pretty intuitive that combining multiple distributions
could better handle abnormalities in data compared to using
just a single one. So, it raises the question: is the
baseline comparison really fair in this case?

2. Also, it would be nice to see if the method is more
task-agnostic.

3. The experiments show that the algorithm can handle data
with outliers. For example, the first experiment suggests
that the proposed method works even with up to 50% outlier
contamination. But looking at Figure 3, it's hard to
clearly see the performance difference between the
generalized method and the Laplace baseline.

4. In the key-point detection experiment, ResNet-18 already
has dropout layers, which provide some uncertainty
handling. The RMSE difference between the proposed method
and the baselines is within 0.02, which doesn’t seem very
significant.

5. It's great that the paper includes a robot grasping
experiment. However, it’s hard to tell how much this
experiment adds, since it appears to just use motion
planning based on detected keypoints. Uncertainty could
also exist in the trajectory itself, not just at the
keypoint level. It would be stronger if the authors used
the proposed loss to train an actual grasping policy. That
would better demonstrate how uncertainty is handled both in
keypoint detection and in the trajectory or policy space.
